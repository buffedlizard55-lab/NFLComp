## 0. What this change set does

Three passes have been run over this platform: **Build**, **Adversarial Audit**
and this **Full Recheck**. Each pass adds corrections rather than restating the
previous pass's numbers; every current figure lives in the generated block above
and in `data/`, and the audit fails if prose disagrees with them.

### Pass 1 — Build
Initial platform: strategy registry, walk-forward engine, ledger, paper-trading
slate, GitHub Pages site, audit suite. Counts from that pass are historical and
are deliberately not restated here.

### Pass 2 — Adversarial Audit
- Data-source registry expanded with the sources discovered by autonomous research
  (nflverse-data Parquet releases, Kalshi trade tape, Polymarket CLOB, Betfair
  Exchange, FTN/SumerSports/PFF-adjacent feeds, COVID crowd natural experiment),
  each carrying its probe result rather than an assumed success.
- New engines: `betting_markets.py` (market taxonomy, price movement, CLV),
  `cross_validation.py` (line-source reconciliation with preserved
  discrepancies), `forward_testing.py`, `execution_engine.py`,
  `autonomous_research.py`, `public_strategy_research.py`.
- Strategy roster extended with the personas the research loop produced, each
  declared with TRAIN 2000-2019 / VALIDATE 2020-2022 / HOLDOUT 2023-2025 windows.
- Site and audit suite extended; every numeric claim it shows bound to data.

### Pass 3 — Full Recheck (this change set)

**Correctness fixes**

1. **Ledger prices are now internally consistent.** Every strategy that recorded
   an implied probability or an edge used a hand-typed `0.5238` (the -110
   default) instead of deriving it from the price actually written to the ledger.
   Across the published ledger that made the recorded edge disagree with the
   recorded price on the majority of bets. `engine/strategies.py` now derives
   `implied_prob` and `edge` from the side's own price, and the audit re-derives
   both on every published record (`LEDGER_PRICE_IMPLIED_PROB_CONSISTENCY`,
   `LEDGER_EDGE_CONSISTENCY`). Because edge feeds the CLV fallback and the
   leaderboard's average edge, the whole simulation was re-run and the ledger
   regenerated.
2. **The published audit file now describes the published documents.** The
   pipeline wrote `data/audit_checks.json` before re-rendering the docs that quote
   it, so the checked-in check file could record failures that the same run had
   already fixed. `scripts/run_pipeline.py` now iterates audit → render → verify
   to a fixpoint and only stops when a round changes no document and the
   read-only audit passes.
3. **Research claims are re-derived or labelled.** `engine/empirical_studies.py`
   recomputes what the game-level snapshot actually supports (wind against
   totals, Thursday rest, divisional high totals, home-underdog ATS, dome
   against outdoor totals) with sample sizes, Wilson intervals and flat-stake
   results, and classifies every entry in `data/research_experiments.json` as
   `DERIVED_DATA` or `DECLARED_ASSUMPTION`. Where a declared number disagrees
   with the snapshot, both values are kept, the dossier entry is marked
   `DISPUTED_BY_SNAPSHOT` and an irregularity is logged.

   The re-derivation corrected a sign error in the study loader itself: the raw
   snapshot's `spread_line` is positive when the home team is favoured, so a
   cover-rate study that does not negate it inverts every result. Under the
   corrected convention home teams cover 48.97% of decided games; the incorrect
   one reported 60.06%.

**New analytics**

4. **`engine/risk_analytics.py`** — per-persona Sharpe and Sortino on the
   per-bet return series, profit factor, payoff ratio, longest losing streak,
   drawdown duration, implied full- and quarter-Kelly stakes against the stake
   actually used, and a seeded bootstrap giving a 95% ROI interval and the
   probability of a 25% drawdown; plus Brier score, log loss and a decile
   reliability table for the model probabilities on every settled bet, and
   portfolio concentration (HHI, largest-persona share, per-season PnL
   correlations). Reported per persona only above a 100-settled-bet floor.
5. **`engine/narrative.py`** — the executive summary, the full strategy roster,
   the key-results section and the data-source table in `README.md` are now
   generated from `data/`, and a prose lint fails the audit when a hand-typed
   quantity contradicts the data. This is what removed the previous README's
   stale claims (a persona count and wager total that no longer existed, and a
   "top persona" that was not on the leaderboard).

**Not fixed, and stated rather than hidden**

- Historical player-prop lines, alternate lines, live prices and archived Kalshi
  order books remain unavailable, so those personas stay forward-test only.
- Several upstream providers are unreachable from this environment; the registry
  records the failed probe.
- `as_of_date` is the last completed game in the snapshot, not "today".
- The dossier's disputed claim is retained with both numbers; it is not retuned
  and not deleted.
- A bootstrap interval that contains zero means the persona's history is
  consistent with break-even. No persona here has demonstrated a durable edge.

## 2. What is verified (facts)

Each item is reproducible with the command in §6.

* **Snapshot integrity.** `data/source/` files are hashed in
  `data/ledger_manifest.json`; the generated evidence block in
  `docs/VERIFICATION.md` lists the SHA-256 of each snapshot file as it exists in
  this repository.
* **Ledger chain.** The published ledger re-derives link by link
  (`engine.ledger.verify_chain`); tampering with any field breaks the chain, and
  both the Python and Node test suites assert that it does.
* **Arithmetic.** Every settled record is re-checked against its own price and
  result (`PNL_CALCULATION_ACCURACY`), and spreads/totals are re-settled from
  the recorded score (`SCORE_MARGIN_ARITHMETIC`, unit tests in
  `engine.settlement`).
* **Chronology.** Games are ordered by date and a bet's decision timestamp never
  falls after the game it prices (`test/engine.test.py`).
* **Reproducibility.** Two consecutive full pipeline runs produced identical
  published-ledger digests and identical summary figures (§6).
* **Numbers on the site.** `index.html` claims and leaderboard rows reconcile
  with `data/summary.json`, `data/bets_ledger.json` and `data/registry.json`.

## 3. What is assumed or unverified (never presented as fact)

* **Sportsbook execution realism.** Historical fills are modelled from recorded
  closing/initial lines with a fixed vig and a liquidity bound. This is an
  *assumption*, not an observed fill, and the ledger labels the provider as
  `Pinnacle/Market Consensus` rather than claiming a ticket.
* **Weather inputs.** Wind/temperature come from the snapshot's game-time
  observations and proxies; they are not stadium sensor readings.
* **Injury inputs.** The checked-in injury JSON is a snapshot; designation
  timing relative to kickoff is asserted, not independently audited against the
  league's publication clock.
* **Blocked providers.** ESPN, Rotowire, NFL.com, TheOddsAPI, NWS and the
  nflverse release assets could not be reached from this environment; the
  registry records the probe result rather than a fabricated success.
* **Edge.** No strategy here has demonstrated a durable edge. Results are
  in-sample histories plus forward paper signals; nothing is a prediction of
  future profit.

## 4. Strategy research status

`data/strategy_lab.json` is the deterministic research report. It is regenerated
by `python3 -m engine.strategy_lab` and `--check` proves the checked-in report
matches the source snapshot. Candidates are *not* judged on win rate alone: the
report includes ROI, drawdown, per-season splits and sample size, and candidates
that fail the holdout stay archived with their unchanged rules (no retuning).

The 2026 slate is forward-only: signals are queued as `FORWARD_TEST` /
`PAPER_TRADE` with `READY_TO_BET`/`QUALIFIED`/`WATCHING` statuses and are settled
only from observed final scores once games complete.

## 5. Ledger, published window and immutability

The complete simulation record and the GitHub Pages export differ by design:
the export carries recent seasons, and everything outside that window is
summarised — with its own totals — in `data/ledger_manifest.json` and on each
leaderboard row. Corrections are never applied in place: a changed record would
break the chain, and the audit would fail. If a correction is ever required, the
protocol is a new record plus an entry in `data/irregularities.json`; nothing in
this repository silently rewrites history.

## 7. Residual risks and open irregularities

1. **Model-vs-market realism.** Multiplicative vig and bounded liquidity are
   approximations of a book's actual price ladder.
2. **Unavailable markets.** Player props, alternate lines, futures, halves and
   quarters, and live prices are forward-test only; their backtests are
   explicitly not run against invented prices.
3. **Cross-source disagreement is recorded, not resolved.** Where the snapshot
   reconciler finds conflicting closing lines, the disagreement rate is published
   and the row is not silently preferred.
4. **Provenance depth.** Local snapshot hashes are verified; the upstream commit
   for each provider could not be re-fetched from this environment for every
   source, and the registry says which ones those are.
5. **No profitability claim.** The competition objective is to maximise simulated
   returns while tracking risk and drawdown; the reported PnL is a record of a
   simulation, not evidence of an exploitable edge.

## 8. Competition objective and honest scoring

Strategies are ranked on simulated PnL with drawdown, ROI, bet count and
per-season splits alongside. High-volatility strategies are retained rather than
filtered out, and no strategy is promoted on a small sample: the research lab
requires a declared holdout and a sample gate, and failing candidates remain
visible with their failure recorded.
