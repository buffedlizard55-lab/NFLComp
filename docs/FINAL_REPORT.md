## 0. What this PR changed

This revision is a *verification* pass over the platform, not a feature pass.
The platform (strategy roster, walk-forward backtest, paper ledger, Kalshi
paper desk, GitHub Pages site) already existed; the defects below meant some
published claims could not be re-derived from the repository.

1. **The published ledger was a silent slice.** `data/bets_ledger.json` carried
   only the 2020+ seasons while `summary.json` and `leaderboard.json` published
   all-time totals over the whole simulation. A reader could not reconcile the
   two. Now `data/ledger_manifest.json` records the window, both totals, the
   chain head and the snapshot hashes, each leaderboard row carries
   `published_ledger_bets` / `published_ledger_pnl` / `pnl_outside_published_window`,
   and the audit reconciles all three files (`LEDGER_MANIFEST_RECONCILIATION`,
   `LEADERBOARD_LEDGER_RECONCILIATION`).
2. **The hash chain was written but never verified**, and the writer had a
   `try/except` that substituted 64 zero bytes on failure — a silent way to fake
   continuity. The chain is now built through `engine.ledger.chain_hash`, a
   failure is fatal, and `engine.ledger.verify_chain` re-derives every link from
   the published bytes (`LEDGER_HASH_CHAIN`, plus unit and Node tests that
   tamper with a record and require detection).
3. **Checked-in exports were stale relative to the code.** The committed data
   mixed an earlier revision of the evidence gates with the current engine. The
   exports in this revision are produced by `python3 scripts/run_pipeline.py`
   from the current code, and the run is byte-reproducible (see §6).
4. **Prose carried hand-typed numbers.** `README.md`, `index.html` and
   `docs/VERIFICATION.md` advertised counts (strategy personas, ledger size,
   upcoming signals, "9/9 audit checks", 40-character strings labelled SHA-256)
   that the data did not support. Those numbers are now generated
   (`scripts/render_readme.py`, `scripts/render_verification.py`,
   `scripts/render_claims.py`) and checked by the audit
   (`PUBLISHED_README_BLOCK`, `PUBLISHED_REPORT_BLOCK`,
   `PUBLISHED_VERIFICATION_BLOCK`, `PUBLISHED_SITE_CLAIMS`).
5. **There was no single reproducible entry point.** `scripts/run_pipeline.py`
   now performs load → walk-forward simulate → export → regenerate docs →
   audit, in two passes so the audit checks the artifacts that were just
   written. `.github/workflows/tests.yml` runs both test suites, the audit and
   every generated-document check on pull requests.

Known and **not fixed** in this PR (carried forward as irregularities, not
hidden): historical player-prop lines, alternate lines and live prices remain
unavailable, so those strategies are forward-test only; several upstream feeds
are unreachable from this environment; and `as_of_date` follows the last
completed game in the snapshot, which is not the same thing as "today".

## 2. What is verified (facts)

Each item is reproducible with the command in §6.

* **Snapshot integrity.** `data/source/` files are hashed in
  `data/ledger_manifest.json`; the generated evidence block in
  `docs/VERIFICATION.md` lists the SHA-256 of each snapshot file as it exists in
  this repository.
* **Ledger chain.** The published ledger re-derives link by link
  (`engine.ledger.verify_chain`); tampering with any field breaks the chain, and
  both the Python and Node test suites assert that it does.
* **Arithmetic.** Every settled record is re-checked against its own price and
  result (`PNL_CALCULATION_ACCURACY`), and spreads/totals are re-settled from
  the recorded score (`SCORE_MARGIN_ARITHMETIC`, unit tests in
  `engine.settlement`).
* **Chronology.** Games are ordered by date and a bet's decision timestamp never
  falls after the game it prices (`test/engine.test.py`).
* **Reproducibility.** Two consecutive full pipeline runs produced identical
  published-ledger digests and identical summary figures (§6).
* **Numbers on the site.** `index.html` claims and leaderboard rows reconcile
  with `data/summary.json`, `data/bets_ledger.json` and `data/registry.json`.

## 3. What is assumed or unverified (never presented as fact)

* **Sportsbook execution realism.** Historical fills are modelled from recorded
  closing/initial lines with a fixed vig and a liquidity bound. This is an
  *assumption*, not an observed fill, and the ledger labels the provider as
  `Pinnacle/Market Consensus` rather than claiming a ticket.
* **Weather inputs.** Wind/temperature come from the snapshot's game-time
  observations and proxies; they are not stadium sensor readings.
* **Injury inputs.** The checked-in injury JSON is a snapshot; designation
  timing relative to kickoff is asserted, not independently audited against the
  league's publication clock.
* **Blocked providers.** ESPN, Rotowire, NFL.com, TheOddsAPI, NWS and the
  nflverse release assets could not be reached from this environment; the
  registry records the probe result rather than a fabricated success.
* **Edge.** No strategy here has demonstrated a durable edge. Results are
  in-sample histories plus forward paper signals; nothing is a prediction of
  future profit.

## 4. Strategy research status

`data/strategy_lab.json` is the deterministic research report. It is regenerated
by `python3 -m engine.strategy_lab` and `--check` proves the checked-in report
matches the source snapshot. Candidates are *not* judged on win rate alone: the
report includes ROI, drawdown, per-season splits and sample size, and candidates
that fail the holdout stay archived with their unchanged rules (no retuning).

The 2026 slate is forward-only: signals are queued as `FORWARD_TEST` /
`PAPER_TRADE` with `READY_TO_BET`/`QUALIFIED`/`WATCHING` statuses and are settled
only from observed final scores once games complete.

## 5. Ledger, published window and immutability

The complete simulation record and the GitHub Pages export differ by design:
the export carries recent seasons, and everything outside that window is
summarised — with its own totals — in `data/ledger_manifest.json` and on each
leaderboard row. Corrections are never applied in place: a changed record would
break the chain, and the audit would fail. If a correction is ever required, the
protocol is a new record plus an entry in `data/irregularities.json`; nothing in
this repository silently rewrites history.

## 7. Residual risks and open irregularities

1. **Model-vs-market realism.** Multiplicative vig and bounded liquidity are
   approximations of a book's actual price ladder.
2. **Unavailable markets.** Player props, alternate lines, futures, halves and
   quarters, and live prices are forward-test only; their backtests are
   explicitly not run against invented prices.
3. **Cross-source disagreement is recorded, not resolved.** Where the snapshot
   reconciler finds conflicting closing lines, the disagreement rate is published
   and the row is not silently preferred.
4. **Provenance depth.** Local snapshot hashes are verified; the upstream commit
   for each provider could not be re-fetched from this environment for every
   source, and the registry says which ones those are.
5. **No profitability claim.** The competition objective is to maximise simulated
   returns while tracking risk and drawdown; the reported PnL is a record of a
   simulation, not evidence of an exploitable edge.

## 8. Competition objective and honest scoring

Strategies are ranked on simulated PnL with drawdown, ROI, bet count and
per-season splits alongside. High-volatility strategies are retained rather than
filtered out, and no strategy is promoted on a small sample: the research lab
requires a declared holdout and a sample gate, and failing candidates remain
visible with their failure recorded.
